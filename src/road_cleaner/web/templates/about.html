How it works

Detection is the easy part.

Plenty of things can spot debris on a road. The hard part is everything that comes after seeing it — working out whose road it is, saying so through the right channel, and then actually checking whether anybody came. That's the part nobody has built, so that's what Road Cleaner is.

The loop · runs per frame, continuously

Seven steps, and only one of them is looking at a picture.

1

Watch

Poll public DOT cameras on a schedule that reflects how much is happening. Busy corridors every couple of minutes, quiet rural cameras every ten, and any camera with an open case every minute — that's the one somebody is waiting on. A frame identical to the last one costs nothing.

2

Detect

A small, cheap model looks first and answers one question: is anything odd here at all? Most pictures of a road are an empty road, and they stop here. Whatever survives goes to the vision model for a real answer — what it is, where in the frame, how bad, how sure. It is asked for a box, not a lane number: from a camera pointed down a road you cannot count the lanes to your left, and a guess dressed as a measurement ends up in a report to a road crew.

3

Confirm

One frame is a maybe, not a yes. A hazard has to still be there ninety seconds later, on a genuinely separate look. Then it gets checked against the state's own incident feed — because if the DOT already posted it, there is nothing to add. Finally the bar to report scales with severity: a person walking on an interstate clears at lower confidence than a bit of debris, because the cost of being wrong isn't symmetrical.

4

Resolve

Work out whose road it actually is. A signal head on a US route inside Durham belongs to the city, not the state. A mattress on Florida's Turnpike belongs to the Turnpike Enterprise, not to the FDOT district the road runs through. Rules answer nearly all of this; the rare genuinely ambiguous case goes to a model, and if that can't decide either, the case is held rather than misfiled.

5

Report

Compose something a maintenance desk will act on — what, where, when, and the marked still — and send it the way that agency accepts. Open311 where a city supports it, the maintenance form where a DOT publishes one, plain structured email everywhere else. Then keep the reference number.

6

Check back

This is the part that makes it worth building. Where there is a fixed camera to go back to, compare against the original evidence photo; if the road is clear, close the case with a before and after. The checks get further apart as a case ages — but closer together when it's running late. A dashcam pass has nothing to return to, and the case says so rather than implying a watch that cannot happen.

7

Push

If the deadline passes and it's still there, file again, one tier up. If it passes twice, stop. An agent that politely re-sends the same message forever is just spam with better manners, so at that point it stops writing to the agency and flags the case for a person instead.

Where it looks

Whatever is pointed at the road.

The model does not care where a frame came from, so neither does the pipeline. What changes between sources is not the detection — it is how much you can do afterwards.

A dashcamA phone on a windscreen, or a clip from one. Finds the hazard and files it, with coordinates a crew can drive to. You pass once, so there is nothing to go back to.
A fixed cameraA public DOT camera at a known location. The same detection, plus the part nobody else does: it goes back, compares against the evidence frame, and escalates until the road is clear.
Whose roadEighteen agencies across GA, FL and NC today, matched on rules rather than guessed — the toll authority before the state DOT, the city before the state for a signal head.
Everywhere elseThe registry is data, not code. Adding a state is adding its DOT and its reporting channel; nothing in the pipeline changes.

House rules

An agent that files paperwork on your behalf had better be a good neighbour.

Every one of these is a constraint on what the system is allowed to do, not a feature list. They exist because the fastest way to make this useless would be for a DOT to start ignoring it.

Quiet by default

Nothing reaches a real agency unless somebody deliberately turns that on. Reports are composed in full and written to disk instead — the same bytes that would have been sent, so there is no untested path waiting to surprise anyone.

It would rather miss one

Two frames, ninety seconds apart, cross-checked against the state's own feed, against a bar that scales with severity. A robot that cries wolf at a maintenance crew is worse than no robot at all.

Roads, not people

Roads and what is on them. No faces, no plates, no tracking anything. Frames are kept as evidence for a week and then deleted. Every filed report keeps its stills, its reasoning and its timestamps, so any agency could audit it.
